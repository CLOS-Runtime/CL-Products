# EP01 COLD-VIEWER YOUTUBE READINESS — FROZEN EVALUATION INSTRUMENT

MARKING: EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE_NOT_GOVERNANCE
STATUS: FROZEN 2026-08-10, before any evaluative reading of the input script. Not modified afterward.

Freeze note: the input script arrived in the same message as the experiment brief, so orchestrator-level literal blindness is impossible. This instrument is therefore built strictly from the criterion list named in the brief, with generic anchors that would apply to any script in this genre. No threshold below references the content, numbers, structure, or wording of the input script beyond what the experiment brief itself names (the brief itself names a "−$90 reveal", a "five-check structure", and "five yellows"; those references appear only where the brief requires a probe about them). The seven evaluators are instantiated in separate, isolated contexts after this file is written.

---

## 0. Fixed assumptions and procedures

- **Narration pace:** 150 wpm nominal (145–155 band) for all timestamp mapping.
- **Production assumption (stated to all evaluators):** competent neutral production — clear narration, clean visuals, on-screen numbers during any arithmetic, no personality performance beyond the words. Evaluators judge the script, not hypothetical production flaws. Production cost is not evaluated.
- **Continuation semantics:** at each sequential checkpoint the evaluator reports CONTINUE (a viewer under their condition would still be watching and would start the next segment) or EXIT (would have left at or before this point). First EXIT is permanent for survival accounting; an exited evaluator keeps evaluating later segments analytically, marked HYPOTHETICAL.
- **Evaluators are lenses, not characters.** No invented biographies, no theatrical reactions. Honest behavioral prediction over kindness or harshness. Skepticism is a posture of scrutiny, not automatic rejection.
- **Independence:** seven evaluators in separate contexts; none sees another's assessments. They may receive this instrument's criterion anchors and label definitions. To avoid priming naturalistic checkpoint behavior, criterion anchors are supplied only at the final (rating/attention-map) step; checkpoint questions before that are behavioral only. This sequencing decision is frozen here, in advance.
- **Criterion-rating aggregation:** each evaluator rates C01–C20 on STRONG/ADEQUATE/WEAK/FAIL; aggregate = median of seven (S=3, A=2, W=1, F=0; median is the 4th order statistic).
- **Attention-map aggregation thresholds (fixed):** a passage counts as consensus FRICTION only if ≥3/7 mark FRICTION (or worse) independently; consensus EXIT_RISK only if ≥2/7 mark EXIT_RISK; consensus PULL only if ≥4/7 mark PULL. Isolated preferences are not findings.
- **Cadence probe (asked of all evaluators at map time, required by the brief):** "Did the multi-check/section structure create a repetitive cadence? YES / SOMEWHAT / NO — one line why."
- **Skeptic probe (Viewer 3 only, at final exposure, required by the brief):** react specifically to (a) lender framing, (b) uncertainty language, (c) disclaimer language, (d) the free-tool CTA.
- **Recall test:** administered after full exposure, without re-showing any text, and before the full text is re-displayed for attention mapping. Evaluators answer from immediate impression; anything they could only reconstruct by re-deriving rather than remembering must be reported PARTIAL or LOST. Items 1–2 of the brief's recall list score as FACTUAL recall; items 3–6 as CONCEPTUAL recall. Exact non-central dollar figures are not required for RETAINED if the underlying mechanism is retained. Per-item scale: RETAINED / PARTIAL / LOST.
- **Recall limitation (recorded at freeze time):** evaluator agents retain transcript context; literal forgetting cannot be enforced. Recall results measure immediate-impression retention plus structural-memorability judgment, scored conservatively, and are reported with this caveat.
- **Checkpoint answers are final once given.** Later material may not retroactively alter earlier checkpoint responses.
- **Payoff tally:** at the central-reveal checkpoint, "Did this feel like a payoff?" is answered YES / PARTIAL / NO.

## 1. Sequential checkpoint schedule (fixed)

1. CP1 at ~0:30 of narration.
2. CP2 at ~1:30 of narration (segment boundary may land at the nearest natural structural seam within ±10s; actual boundary recorded in the run log).
3. CP3 immediately at the script's central numerical reveal, before any subsequent text.
4. CP4 after the next two analysis sections.
5. CP5 after the following two analysis sections.
6. CP6 after the ending block (synthesis, CTA, next-episode setup).

## 2. Criteria C01–C20 with frozen anchors

Each anchor is written from a single evaluator's experiential standpoint so evaluators can rate independently.

**C01. Cold-open effectiveness (first ~30s)**
- STRONG: by 0:30 you hold a specific question you actively want answered and could state exactly what the video is about; no urge to check the seek bar.
- ADEQUATE: you know what the video is about and would keep watching, though the pull is mild.
- WEAK: subject unclear or hook generic; you'd continue only on channel trust a cold viewer doesn't have.
- FAIL: you would leave or scrub within the first 30 seconds.

**C02. Curiosity after 30 seconds**
- STRONG: curiosity higher than at 0:00; you are already predicting answers.
- ADEQUATE: flat but sufficient; you'd give it another minute.
- WEAK: declining; continuing is inertia, not interest.
- FAIL: gone; you'd exit.

**C03. Curiosity after ~90 seconds**
- STRONG: curiosity has risen since 0:30 because the video added a reason to stay that wasn't present at the open.
- ADEQUATE: curiosity held flat; still a live question.
- WEAK: curiosity decreased; you're waiting for it to get to the point.
- FAIL: you'd exit at or before ~90s.

**C04. Clarity of premise**
- STRONG: by ~1:30 you can state the decision, who is deciding, and the format in one effortless sentence.
- ADEQUATE: you can state the subject; minor fuzziness about scope or format.
- WEAK: you would need a rewind to say what is being decided.
- FAIL: premise misunderstood or opaque.

**C05. Stakes**
- STRONG: you can say concretely what the people deciding stand to gain/lose and why it matters to them; consequential without exaggeration.
- ADEQUATE: stakes stated and believed, but felt mildly.
- WEAK: stakes asserted rather than felt, or generic.
- FAIL: no discernible stakes.

**C06. Information density**
- STRONG: nearly every sentence adds information or advances the decision; nothing you'd skip.
- ADEQUATE: occasional slack; nowhere would you skip more than a few seconds.
- WEAK: at least one stretch (~20s+) that repeats or pads; you'd be tempted to skip ahead.
- FAIL: you'd scrub through significant portions.

**C07. Cognitive load**
- STRONG: you could follow every number and step in one listen at narration speed (with on-screen numbers) without pausing.
- ADEQUATE: one or two moments need effort but the thread never breaks.
- WEAK: you lose the arithmetic or logic somewhere and continue without understanding.
- FAIL: sustained confusion; you'd abandon the reasoning.

**C08. Narrative progression**
- STRONG: each section changes the situation or your understanding — you can name what changed after each; a story advancing, not items ticked.
- ADEQUATE: mostly progressive; one section feels static or interchangeable.
- WEAK: two or more sections feel like a list being completed.
- FAIL: after the midpoint, sections feel skippable without loss.

**C09. Trust / credibility**
- STRONG: you believe the narrator is straight about numbers, uncertainty, and motive; the caveats increase confidence.
- ADEQUATE: broadly credible; a moment or two feels performed or salesy.
- WEAK: you suspect motivated reasoning, a hidden agenda, or unearned certainty somewhere.
- FAIL: you conclude the video exists to sell you something or the numbers are constructed for effect.

**C10. Unnecessary qualification**
- STRONG: qualifiers are few and each does work; the narrator commits where commitment is honest.
- ADEQUATE: hedging noticeable but not attention-draining.
- WEAK: hedges/disclaimers recur enough to register as a pattern and slow the video.
- FAIL: qualification undermines the point of watching; no usable claim survives.

**C11. Repetition**
- STRONG: nothing repeated without new consequence; any recap lands at a natural consolidation point.
- ADEQUATE: minor restatement, arguably useful.
- WEAK: you notice "this was already said" at least twice.
- FAIL: structural repetition creates a predictable cycle you want to skip.

**C12. Emotional / human specificity**
- STRONG: at least one concrete human detail makes the deciders feel real, integrated with the reasoning rather than decorating it.
- ADEQUATE: human presence felt but general.
- WEAK: the people feel like a framing device for a spreadsheet.
- FAIL: no humans discernible behind the decision.

**C13. Usefulness**
- STRONG: you leave with a method or distinction you could apply to your own decision, stated clearly enough to reuse.
- ADEQUATE: you leave with a general lesson.
- WEAK: interesting but not transferable, or transferable but never shown.
- FAIL: nothing usable.

**C14. Midpoint momentum**
- STRONG: in the middle third you still hold an open question you care about, and the video keeps raising the cost of not knowing.
- ADEQUATE: momentum dips but a live question remains.
- WEAK: you continue out of politeness/completeness; the destination feels known.
- FAIL: you'd exit in the middle third.

**C15. Strength of the central reveal**
- STRONG: the key numerical/analytical moment lands as a genuine reframe — surprising yet inevitable in hindsight; you'd remember it tomorrow.
- ADEQUATE: a clear payoff with modest surprise.
- WEAK: the payoff feels procedural — a calculation completed rather than a belief changed.
- FAIL: no identifiable payoff moment.

**C16. Post-reveal momentum**
- STRONG: immediately after the biggest payoff the video hands you a new specific reason to keep watching; what follows feels like rising action, not appendix.
- ADEQUATE: a reason exists but is weaker than the pre-reveal pull; mild sag.
- WEAK: material after the reveal feels like additional information after the answer already arrived.
- FAIL: you would stop at or shortly after the reveal.

**C17. Satisfaction of the ending**
- STRONG: the ending reframes or consolidates the journey so an unresolved outcome still feels like an arrival; you can say what was gained.
- ADEQUATE: clear and acceptable; modest closure.
- WEAK: trails off, or asserts significance the viewer doesn't feel.
- FAIL: feels evasive — the video withheld an answer it implied it would give.

**C18. CTA fit**
- STRONG: the offered next step feels like the natural continuation of what you watched — same method, same spirit; you might actually click.
- ADEQUATE: reasonable and unobtrusive; you wouldn't resent it.
- WEAK: a noticeable gear-shift into marketing; trust dips.
- FAIL: retroactively recasts the video as a funnel.

**C19. Next-episode desire**
- STRONG: you can name the specific question you want the next episode to answer; you'd click its card.
- ADEQUATE: you'd plausibly watch another if surfaced, without a burning question.
- WEAK: indifference; done with this story.
- FAIL: aversion; one episode was enough or trust broke.

**C20. Overall YouTube readiness (holistic)**
- STRONG: you'd expect this to retain a cold audience; you personally would finish it and consider subscribing.
- ADEQUATE: publishable; competent; most viewers who start it finish it, some convert.
- WEAK: predictable meaningful drop-off or trust damage; needs work before publishing.
- FAIL: not publishable as a first episode.

## 3. Attention labels (fixed definitions)

- **PULL** — actively increases the desire to keep watching (curiosity, stakes, surprise, payoff).
- **CARRY** — neutral forward motion; neither adds nor drains attention.
- **FRICTION** — effortful, repetitive, defensive, or slack; drains attention but would not alone cause an exit.
- **EXIT_RISK** — a moment where a viewer under this condition might actually stop watching.

Each passage receives exactly one label per evaluator.

## 4. Frozen dispositions and decision rules

Five dispositions (exactly one will be chosen):
READY_AS_WRITTEN · READY_WITH_MICRO_EDITS · READY_WITH_BOUNDED_CUTS · STRUCTURAL_REVISION_REQUIRED · WRONG_EPISODE_ONE

Definitions used by the rules:
- "Consensus FRICTION/EXIT_RISK/PULL" per §0 thresholds.
- "Checkpoint survival" = count of non-hypothetical CONTINUE at each of the six checkpoints.
- "Median" = criterion median per §0.

**Decision rules (applied mechanically to the aggregated evidence; where more than one disposition is consistent with all binding constraints, choose the mildest; record near-misses):**

- **READY_AS_WRITTEN** requires ALL of: survival ≥6/7 at every checkpoint; zero consensus EXIT_RISK passages; ≤1 consensus FRICTION passage; no criterion median WEAK or FAIL; central-reveal payoff YES ≥5/7; factual+conceptual recall of the core mechanism and central numerical result RETAINED by ≥6/7; C18 median ≥ ADEQUATE with the skeptical evaluator personally ≥ ADEQUATE on C09.
- **READY_WITH_MICRO_EDITS** requires ALL of: survival ≥5/7 at every checkpoint and no checkpoint with ≥3 EXITs; consensus EXIT_RISK confined to ≤1 passage, addressable by line-level CUT/COMPRESS/CLARIFY; consensus FRICTION ≤3 passages, none requiring reordering or section removal; total edit budget ≲5% of words with no reordering; ≤2 criterion medians WEAK and none FAIL; payoff YES ≥5/7 (PARTIAL counts half).
- **READY_WITH_BOUNDED_CUTS** requires ALL of: section order and section inventory sound (no evidence that reordering or re-conceiving sections is needed); consensus FRICTION/EXIT_RISK concentrated in identifiable passages or subsections whose removal/heavy compression (≲25% of words, connective tissue only, no new substantive prose) would plausibly resolve the observed checkpoint losses and sag; ≤4 criterion medians WEAK; no FAIL median on premise (C04), trust (C09), ending (C17); survival never below 4/7.
- **STRUCTURAL_REVISION_REQUIRED** if ANY of: survival ≤3/7 at any checkpoint; FAIL median on any of C04/C09/C16/C17; majority evidence (≥4/7) locating the same structural cause — wrong reveal placement, wrong section order, or a repetitive architecture that cuts alone cannot fix without rebuilding sections.
- **WRONG_EPISODE_ONE** if the episode may function as content but fails first-episode functions specifically: <4/7 can state what the channel offers; or next-episode desire ≤2/7 while ending satisfaction median ≤ WEAK; or comprehension requires context a first episode cannot assume; or evaluator+adversary evidence converges that the material's job belongs later in a sequence rather than at the entry point.

Order of application: test READY_AS_WRITTEN first, then READY_WITH_MICRO_EDITS, then READY_WITH_BOUNDED_CUTS; STRUCTURAL_REVISION_REQUIRED and WRONG_EPISODE_ONE are triggered by their conditions at any point and override milder dispositions if their conditions are met.

## 5. Phase separation (fixed)

- The adversary is instantiated only after all seven cold-viewer runs complete; it receives the script (and passage IDs) only — not the viewers' outputs; every criticism must quote or precisely locate script evidence; generic platform advice is prohibited; its output cannot add "findings" to the attention map unless corroborated there.
- The season-arc evaluator is a fresh context, receives the season fact, and cannot change cold-viewer scores.
- Minimum-change interventions (CUT/COMPRESS/MOVE/CLARIFY/NONE) may cite only defects supported by the experiment: consensus attention flags, checkpoint survival losses, recall failures, criterion medians ≤ WEAK, or adversary points corroborated by viewer data.
- All individual evaluator outputs are preserved verbatim before aggregation.
