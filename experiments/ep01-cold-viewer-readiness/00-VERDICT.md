# PHASE IX — VERDICT
## EP01 Cold-Viewer YouTube Readiness Experiment

**MARKING: EXPERIMENTAL_EVIDENCE_NOT_GOVERNANCE**

This document records experimental evidence only. It is not a governance decision, not an approval, not a publication state, and does not alter any canon, narration authority, production state or existing artifact.

## A. Overall disposition

# READY_WITH_BOUNDED_CUTS

Selected by mechanical application of the frozen decision rules (`01-frozen-instrument.md` §4, applied in `08-disposition-derivation.md`). It is the mildest disposition consistent with every binding constraint.

- **READY_AS_WRITTEN** rejected: requires zero consensus exit-risk passages (found 3), ≤1 consensus friction passage (found 9), and no WEAK criterion median (found 1).
- **READY_WITH_MICRO_EDITS** rejected: requires consensus exit risk confined to ≤1 passage, ≤3 friction passages, and no reordering. Two supported interventions are relocations.
- **STRUCTURAL_REVISION_REQUIRED** not triggered: no checkpoint fell below 7/7; no FAIL median on premise, trust, post-reveal momentum or ending; and the unanimous cadence finding is a constant *verdict token*, not a wrong section order or a wrong reveal placement — the remedies the evaluators themselves proposed are cuts.
- **WRONG_EPISODE_ONE** not triggered: 7/7 stated what the channel offers, 7/7 understood why EP02 exists, 0/7 said the ending was evasive, and comprehension required no assumed context.

The magnitude of change sits at the **light end** of the bounded-cuts band: ~57 words for the minimum set, ~92 including optional items, out of 1,192. The disposition is driven by the *count and distribution* of independently confirmed friction, and by two required relocations — not by the volume of text involved.

## D. Recall — what survived one exposure

**Factual (items 1–2): 14/14 RETAINED.** Every evaluator held both the apparent $700 opportunity and the −$90 result, unprompted.

**Conceptual (items 3–6): 23/28 RETAINED, 5 PARTIAL, 0 LOST.** Items 4, 5 and 6 — the household's current decision, what the channel is, and what happens next — were **21/21 RETAINED**.

**The one systematic failure is item 3**, the five risks: 5/7 PARTIAL. The degradation is not random. All seven retained the reserve/worst-month risk, the next-mortgage kill condition, and "costs ate the margin." Five of seven reported that **tenant-ready costs and personal capacity blur together** — and those are exactly the two checks containing no arithmetic, the same two the attention map flags. V7 names the mechanism: they arrive "without a number… and a story-first viewer stores the shape rather than the components."

Uniformly not retained, and not scored as failures per the frozen rules: the individual line items, the $5,000 reserve, the $1,850 sensitivity. In every case the underlying mechanism survived.

**Verdict on the mental model:** the episode produced a usable, compressed, transferable model rather than mere sentence-by-sentence comprehension. Its one weakness degrades along a predictable axis — the unquantified checks are the ones that fade.

*Limitation, recorded at freeze time: evaluator agents retain transcript context, so literal forgetting cannot be enforced. Scored conservatively; directional.*

## E. The −$90 structural test

**The script does create a second reason to continue after its strongest numerical reveal. This is confirmed, not marginal.**

At the reveal checkpoint, with no later text visible: **payoff 7/7 YES; main question answered 7/7 PARTLY; continue 7/7.** Not one evaluator believed the video had answered its central question, and every one named a specific unresolved pull — most often "why would anyone pay $90 a month to keep this?", the unverified rent flagged at P14, or the four remaining checks.

The mechanism is that the reveal **inverts the question rather than closing it**. V7: "the story got harder, not settled." V3: "the script just conceded the case against its own channel name." V1: "negative cash flow makes the decision more interesting, not less."

Independently, the adversary — which never saw viewer data — concluded the reveal is **CORRECTLY placed** at 2:14 of 7:57. Its objection is not the timing but that P03 and P12 pre-announce the direction, making P20 "a receipt, not a reveal." The viewer data partially supports this: 4/7 rated the reveal STRONG, 3/7 ADEQUATE, with V4, V5 and V7 all noting the direction was telegraphed and only the magnitude was news. That is a real cost — and it is the same cost that buys 7/7 STRONG on trust. The panel's own CP1 responses show why: five of seven cited "I knew that wasn't profit" as the specific line that stopped them clicking away.

## F. The five-yellow test

**Earned, not anticlimactic — by 5/7, with a real dissent.**

MEANINGFUL 5/7 · MIXED 2/7 · **ANTICLIMAX 0/7.**

What converts it is one line, named independently by six evaluators: *"Not a verdict. Conditions."* V6 — the lowest-financial-literacy lens — put it best: it "reframes the non-answer into five plain-English requirements I can actually repeat from memory." The second load-bearing line is P54, *"It has to beat selling"*, which V5 called "the correct decision rule" and V3 said directly answered their opening suspicion that the channel name predetermined the outcome.

The dissent is precise and worth preserving. V4: "a scoring system that returns the same color five times out of five isn't discriminating anything." V7: "all five checks deferred rather than resolved." The adversary sharpens it further: *green* and *red* are never defined, so the viewer has no basis to know why check one — which produced a negative number — is not red.

So: the *result* is earned; the *instrument* did no work. The meaning is carried entirely by the conditions, not by the colors. Five of seven found that sufficient.

## G. Trust — and the skeptical viewer specifically

**C09 Trust: STRONG, 7/7 unanimous.** No evaluator suspected motivated reasoning. The skeptical evaluator finished the video, rated trust STRONG, and would watch EP02.

Its probe, verbatim in substance:

- **Lender framing** — the one weak spot it found: *"The one unexamined authority — 'keep it and buy the next house' means the lender originates a second loan instead of losing one; the script hedges his rules but never names his incentive."* This is the sharpest unaddressed trust observation in the experiment. Below the intervention threshold at 1/7, so not prescribed; recorded because it is the only place a skeptic found an unearned authority.
- **Uncertainty language** — passed, with reasoning: *"the hedges consistently cut against the attractive number (rent sensitivity runs down, never up) — the inverse of guru false precision; reads genuine."*
- **Disclaimer language** — passed with a caveat: *"Partly liability shielding, but gurus universalize because universalizing sells; shrinking claims to 'our file' is anti-sales behavior. Slightly writerly, still earned."* Note the tension with the attention map, where P09 drew 6/7 friction: the disclaimers **buy trust and cost momentum simultaneously**. That is why intervention 5 relocates rather than deletes.
- **Free-tool CTA** — passed, conditionally: *"Structurally a lead magnet — provisional pass only because it follows full delivery and is deflated in the same breath; if that link lands on an email gate or a 'book a call' page, this retroactively becomes the best-built funnel I've seen this year, and I don't come back."*

That last clause is the single most actionable trust finding in the experiment, and it is not about the script at all — **the script's CTA passes; what sits behind the link is what would break it.** All seven rated the CTA EARNED; C18 median STRONG. The adversary's contrary claim is contradicted by every evaluator.

## H. Season function — kept separate from standalone readiness

A fresh evaluator, given the ten-episode season fact and no later scripts, assessed season function only. **This cannot and did not change any cold-viewer score.**

**Verdict: EP01 functions as a season opener, with two identifiable costs.**

It does the essential things: establishes a live question with real stakes, names a repeatable procedure, installs a scoring vocabulary, commits to a distinctive register of restraint, hands off to EP02 out of its own arithmetic rather than by announcement — and, critically, "ends genuinely undecided… which is the one non-negotiable requirement of an opener and which most first episodes quietly violate."

The two costs:

1. **EP01 overspends EP02.** P26, P27, P29 and P31 deliver EP02's mechanism, scenario and lesson, and P28's $5,000 pre-anchors its likely headline number. "It can confirm; it cannot reveal." Note this is a *season* finding that runs directly against the cold-viewer data, where P26 and P29 were unanimous PULL and the EP02 hook was understood by 7/7. The material is EP01's strongest stretch; the objection is that it belongs to the next episode.
2. **The agenda is closed and sized for five, not ten.** The five conditions at P53 fix the list. Six to eight episodes are visible from EP01. The gap to ten closes only via two structures EP01 never signposts: re-scoring yellows toward green or red, and **building the sell-side baseline it asserts at P54 but never numbers** — named as "the single largest structural liability EP01 creates."

Also flagged: no passage tells a subscriber the season is finite or that a decision will be reached, so "a subscriber cannot tell whether they are joining a finite story with a resolution or an open-ended process series." And the acknowledged hole at P21 — taxes and insurance excluded from the anchor number — is a check EP01 wrote that the season must cash.

**Separation statement:** on standalone YouTube readiness the episode's best stretch is P25–P36; on season function that same stretch is the chief liability. Both are true. Neither result was allowed to influence the other.

## J. Strongest evidence against my own verdict

Two opposing cases. Both are real; I find the first stronger.

**The case that this is READY_AS_WRITTEN and the disposition is too harsh.**

Every evaluator watched to the end. Not one exited at any checkpoint under sequential exposure — the condition designed to catch exactly that. The three "exit risks" are *predictions* elicited after full exposure, made by evaluators who had already demonstrated, in real time, that they did not leave. There is a genuine methodological asymmetry here: the attention map asks "where might someone stop?" and inevitably produces answers, while the sequential protocol — the harder, more behaviourally valid test — produced none. Add: zero FAIL criteria, unanimous STRONG on trust, usefulness, premise clarity and cold open, 7/7 payoff, 7/7 earned CTA, 7/7 correct recall of the channel and the next episode, 0/7 finding the ending evasive. A first episode that holds an impatient viewer, a hostile skeptic, and a viewer with no financial vocabulary through eight minutes to a deliberately unresolved ending has done something rare. The strongest form of this objection: **my disposition rests on nine flagged passages totalling under 8% of the words, none of which actually cost a single viewer.**

My answer is that the frozen rules were fixed before exposure precisely so this could not be argued after the fact, and they are unambiguous — three consensus exit-risk locations and nine consensus friction passages exceed both milder bands. But the objection has force, and the honest summary is that this script sits at the boundary between MICRO_EDITS and BOUNDED_CUTS, not in the middle of the latter.

**The case that the disposition is too lenient.**

The adversary's central finding is corroborated and is not fully fixable by cutting: the arithmetic stops at P22, and roughly 69% of the runtime afterward produces one new quantity while twice announcing that numbers are being withheld. Cuts compress that stretch; they cannot fill it. Supporting this: **not one evaluator rated overall readiness STRONG** — C20 was ADEQUATE 7/7, a unanimous verdict of "competent, not exceptional." The cadence finding is unanimous, and the deepest version of it — that the color instrument produced zero discrimination and green and red are never defined — arguably *is* architectural. Four of seven independently marked the top exit risk. And the recall data shows the two unquantified checks failing to survive as distinct items in 5/7 cases, which is a content problem, not a trim problem.

Why I still hold the verdict: the frozen rule for structural revision requires ≥4/7 locating a cause "that cuts alone cannot fix without rebuilding sections." No evaluator proposed reordering or removing a section; 0/7 reported post-answer appendix at any checkpoint; and the one proposed remedy that *would* require rebuilding — giving a check a resolved color — came from a single evaluator and would change the script's substantive finding, which no evidence here licenses.

## K. Final answer

**Would I publish this as Episode 1 of an unknown YouTube channel, if production execution were competent?**

**Yes — after the six minimum interventions, which cost about fifty-seven words and no new material. And if forced to choose today between publishing it exactly as written or holding it back, I would publish it as written.**

The reason is what the failure modes are. Nothing in this experiment showed a viewer losing the thread, disbelieving the narrator, feeling sold to, or feeling cheated by the unresolved ending — the four ways a first episode actually dies. Cognitive load was STRONG even for the viewer with no financial vocabulary. Trust was unanimously STRONG, including from a lens actively hunting for a funnel, which finished the episode and would watch the next one. Seven of seven could state what the channel is and what happens next. The defects the experiment found are **drag, not damage**: repetition at the one-minute mark, two announced-absent numbers, a stretch of unquantified checks, and a scoring device that stops transmitting information around check three.

What earns publication is that the script does the hardest thing well and the easy thing badly. The hard thing — inverting an attractive number in front of the viewer, one honest deduction at a time, and making a *worse* result more interesting than a better one — works on every lens tested, and the adversary conceded it: the ladder is arithmetically clean and a viewer with no finance background can follow all of it. The easy thing — not saying $2,000 minus $1,300 four times, not promising a number twice, not ending five consecutive sections with the same word — is exactly what the minimum interventions fix, at a cost of under 5% of the script.

Two cautions that are not about the script and should not be lost. First, the skeptical evaluator's conditional pass on the CTA: the language is clean, but if the link behind it lands on an email gate or a booking page, the trust this episode builds is spent retroactively and that viewer does not return. **The script's CTA passes; the destination is what would break it.** Second, the season-function finding runs against the cold-viewer finding on the same passage — P25–P36 is the episode's strongest stretch for a standalone viewer and its chief liability for a ten-episode season, because it hands EP02 a question whose insight, scenario, lesson and candidate answer have all already been given. That is a season-planning problem, deliberately kept out of the readiness verdict, and it is the one finding I would want considered before EP02 is written rather than after.
