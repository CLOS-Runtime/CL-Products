## Consensus PULL — 25 of 60 passages

Unanimous 7/7 PULL: **P01, P03, P07, P12, P16, P20, P25, P26, P29, P33, P36, P48, P52, P54, P60.**

That is fifteen passages every single evaluator independently marked as actively increasing the desire to keep watching. They cluster in five places:

1. **The cold open** (P01, P03) — the number, and the pre-emptive "I knew that wasn't profit."
2. **The turn and the frame** (P07, P12) — the 3% lender conversation; "we gave the idea five ways to fail."
3. **The ledger and its result** (P16, P20) — the first deduction and the −$90 landing.
4. **The strongest conceptual run** (P25, P26, P29, P33, P36) — averages, the roof that fails all at once, the November/December vignette, and the financing kill condition. This is the densest continuous PULL block in the script.
5. **The finale's reframes** (P48, P52, P54, P60) — "The truer plan had the worse number"; "Not a verdict. Conditions."; "It has to beat selling"; the EP02 question.

Additional consensus PULL (4–6/7): P06, P14, P19, P22, P23, P41, P42, P45, P47, P57.

## Cadence probe — the five-check structure

**5/7 YES, 2/7 SOMEWHAT, 0/7 NO.** Every evaluator detected the repetitive cadence; none denied it. The convergence on *when* it becomes predictable is unusually tight:

- V1: "by check three I could predict the shape and the verdict before hearing them"
- V3: "fully predictable to me by check three… I had correctly guessed an all-yellow ending by check four"
- V4: "by check three I could predict the exact shape of every remaining section"
- V5: "by check four I could predict the verdict before hearing it"
- V6: "from check three onward I was saying 'yellow' before the narrator did"
- V7: "By the third header I could predict the shape of the next 90 seconds and the exact words that would end it"

The mechanism named repeatedly is not that the *content* repeats — all seven agree each check introduces a genuinely different idea — but that the **verdict token is constant**. V5 states it most precisely: "a scorecard whose output is constant carries zero information." V3 offers the strongest defense: "the uniformity is also what makes 'five yellows' legible as a result, so it costs momentum and buys the ending."
